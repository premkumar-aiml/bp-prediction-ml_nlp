High diastolic blood pressure, the lower number in a blood pressure reading, can significantly increase the risk of cardiovascular disease. It indicates increased pressure in the arteries when the heart is resting between beats, and this sustained pressure can damage blood vessels and impair heart function. This damage can lead to a range of cardiovascular issues, including heart attack, stroke, and heart failure.
Here's a more detailed explanation:
What is Diastolic Blood Pressure?
Diastolic blood pressure is the measurement of the pressure in your arteries when your heart muscle is resting between beats. It's the bottom number in a blood pressure reading (e.g., 120/80 mmHg).
How Diastolic Hypertension Affects Cardiovascular Health
Increased Risk of Heart Attack and Stroke:
High diastolic blood pressure can damage blood vessels, including those supplying the heart and brain, increasing the risk of blockages and subsequent heart attacks and strokes.
Heart Failure:
Sustained high diastolic pressure can put extra strain on the heart, leading to heart failure, particularly diastolic heart failure, where the heart muscle becomes stiff and can't fill properly.
Aortic Aneurysm:
High diastolic pressure can weaken the walls of the aorta, the main artery carrying blood from the heart, increasing the risk of aortic aneurysms (bulges in the artery wall).
Coronary Artery Disease:
Diastolic dysfunction, often associated with high diastolic pressure, can reduce blood flow to the heart muscle, contributing to coronary artery disease.
Why is it Important to Manage Diastolic Blood Pressure?
Managing diastolic blood pressure is crucial for preventing and controlling cardiovascular disease. Lifestyle changes, such as adopting a healthy diet, exercising regularly, and managing stress, can help lower diastolic blood pressure. In some cases, medication may be necessary to control blood pressure and reduce the risk of cardiovascular complications.
